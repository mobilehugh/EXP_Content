= Offensive Peripherals

Offensive peripherals are attacks that the persona can use to inflict damage on targets.
Robotic attacks are __rarely__ baked in weapons like guns or lazers.
They are more often malfunctioning devices that can be weaponized.
Most of the time robots have no offensive peripherals at all.
Robots without offensive peripherals require role playing to inflict damage beyond ramming.

A baked-in weapon is a device built into the robot and designed as a weapon, such as a gun or a combat lazer.
The player uses her Skilled Bonus when attacking with a baked-in weapon.
A misused peripheral is a device designed for a non-combat task that the robot uses as a weapon.
For example, a stapler used to shoot staples at a target is a misused peripheral, even if its attack uses gun damage.
The player uses her Raw Bonus of 0 when attacking with a misused peripheral.
Being built into the robot or generated on an offensive peripheral table does not make a tool a baked-in weapon.
When generating an offensive peripheral, establish whether it is an actual weapon or a misused tool.
Permission to attack without a Free Will check does not itself grant the Skilled Bonus.
Ramming uses the separate progression described below.

All robots can weaponize themselves and ram targets with their chassis.
The robot type determines if there are any additional offensive peripherals.
The robot type fabrication info has some gibberish on the offensive peripherals line.

The referee and players should limit the amount and lethality of robotic offensive peripherals for campaign balance and fun.
A powerful robot can easily acquire a dozen thrusting and striking weapons.
The most dangerous attack table could fashion the robot with a long-range fusion bomb, or worse.
It is okay to limit the impact of powerful weapons creatively.
It is okay to say “enough is enough already.

[[_attack_level_zero]]
== Attack Level 0
These are the robots that have no malfunctioning, intentional or accidentally dangerous weapons built-in.
The player is responsible for role playing their persona's participation in combat.
In fact there may be existing safety features in place that prevent the robot from harming targets.
Robots in attack level 0 may have to make frequent xref:uncombat:robot_free_will.adoc[Free Will,window=_blank, rel="noopener noreferrer"] checks to engage in combat.

=== Creative Combat
If the player wants her persona to smash a target with her lifting arm she would have to explain the action.
If the persona had a striking offensive peripheral she could just attack.
Robots in attack level 0 may have to make frequent xref:uncombat:robot_free_will.adoc[Free Will,window=_blank, rel="noopener noreferrer"] checks to engage in combat.

=== Ramming
All robots can ram themselves into targets.
For large robots this can resemble vehicular homicide.
For smaller robots this can resemble vehicular suicide.
Ramming can also damage the robot.

The robot will assign a particular surface of it's chassis for ramming.
What the surface is shaped like determines the damage type.
All robots have a Ramming rating from 0 to 4.
Higher Ramming ratings make the robot more effective at ramming.

==== Ramming Surface
A robot will only be able to ram opponents with a pre-determined part of its chassis.
This part will be considered the least vital and can withstand the repeated punishment.

[[_ramming_rating]]
==== Ramming
At Ramming 0, the player must win a Free Will check before allowing her robot persona to ram.
At Ramming 1 to 4, this check is not required.
The player must then win an attack roll to inflict damage.
Fabrication determines the robot’s ramming surface and Ramming rating.
The surface table above supplies the damage dice; the rating table below determines the attack bonus and whether the robot takes reciprocal damage.
Resolve speed, the damage roll and size adjustments using xref:combat:combat_and_robots.adoc#_ramming[Robot Combat: Ramming,window=_blank, rel="noopener noreferrer"].
Ramming is a Strike attack.

[[_attack_level_one]]
== Attack Level 1
Most of these attacks are malfunctioning peripherals that can be weaponized by the robot.
Do not underestimate this attack level.
Attack Table 1 can generate a collection of lethal close combat attacks very quickly.
There is also a chance to elevate to Attack Level 2 from this table.
These attacks do not require a player to win a control factor roll to misuse them.

Each time the robot gains another Ramming result at Ramming 4, she moves to a more dangerous ramming surface.
So a robot with a flat (1d4) ramming surface would keep the benefits of Ramming 4 and increase damage to a blunt point (1d6).

[[_attack_level_one_ramming]]
=== Ramming
Increasing Ramming improves the robot’s ability to ram targets.
Each Ramming result increases the rating by 1, up to Ramming 4.
The first result increases the rating from Ramming 0 to Ramming 1.
A robot with Ramming 1 advances to Ramming 2, and so on.

Moving from Ramming 0 to Ramming 1 allows the robot to ram targets without making a xref:uncombat:robot_free_will.adoc[Free Will,window=_blank, rel="noopener noreferrer"] check.
This is a huge leap forward in the realm of smashing things.
This robot still uses her Raw Strike Bonus and still takes damage for the intentional collision.

=== Strike Attack
The robot may use one of her peripherals as a dangerous striking weapon.
If the robot has no obvious peripheral to misuse, it may have the actual weapon for a peripheral.
The striking peripheral is a Strike attack.
A tool for delicately holding vases can smash a nearby target instead.
Multiple visits to striking peripheral are cumulative, and a robot can gather many simultaneous Strike attacks.

The player rolls on the Striking Peripheral Attack Table below to determine what kind of weapon the peripheral most resembles.

=== Multiple Attacks

=== Nausea Attack
The nausea attack temporarily incapacitates organic targets with overwhelming nausea.
This robotic attack is an area of effect weapon.
As with other robot attacks, the nausea attack could arise from misusing a peripheral or a baked-in weapon.
The attack could arise from releasing toxic gases from the robot’s power plant.
The nausea attack uses vapours, sprays or gases, and targets with atmospheric protections are unaffected.

.*Range*
The range of the attack is 1 hex per point of her STR Prime score.
A robot with a 3 STR Prime has a range of 3 hexes from her chassis.

.*Intensity*
The intensity of the attack is random each time it is activated.
The intensity of the attack is 3 to 12 (3d4).
The target must win a saving roll versus poison using her *constitution* or succumb to nausea and vomiting.
The attack has no effect if the target wins her saving roll.
The incapacitation lasts for 0 to 3 units (1d4 - 1).

.*Cumulative*
The nausea attack is cumulative and repeatedly generating this attack makes it stronger.
The first appearance of nausea attack is level 1.
Each level increases the range by 1 hex and the intensity by 1-4 (1d4).

=== Paralysis Attack
The paralysis attack temporarily incapacitates organic targets with overwhelming weakness.
This robotic attack is an area of effect weapon.
As with other robot attacks, the paralysis attack could arise from misusing a peripheral or a baked-in weapon.
The attack could arise from releasing a damaging energy blast from the robot’s power plant.
The paralysis attack uses waves, pulsations, and harmonics to penetrate atmospheric protections like vac suits.

.*Range*
The range of the attack is 1 hex per point of her INT Prime score.
A robot with a 3 INT Prime has a range of 3 hexes from her chassis.

.*Intensity*
The intensity of the attack is random each time it is activated.
The intensity of the attack is 3 to 12 (3d4).
The target must win a saving roll versus poison using her *dexterity* or succumb to incapacitation weakness.
The attack has no effect if the target wins her saving roll.
The paralysis lasts for 0 to 3 units (1d4 - 1).

.*Cumulative*
The paralysis attack is cumulative and repeatedly generating this attack makes it stronger.
The first appearance of paralysis attack is level 1.
Each level increases the range by 1 hex and the intensity by 1-4 (1d4).

=== Toxic Jet
The toxic jet is a blast of nastiness that can damage both organic and inorganic targets.
The name is intentionally generic as the referee and player will decide if the attack is acidic, alkali, pyro (heat) or cryo (cold).
Only in very particular milieu or story situations will the type of toxic jet matter.

The attack has a range of 1 hex per point of Dexterity.
The toxic jet is a fling attack, and the player must win an attack roll to inflict damage.
A toxic jet inflicts 1-8 (1d8) HPS of damage per point of combined STR_P and CON_P (primes).
There are no damage bonuses for this attack.

[[_attack_level_four]]
== Attack Level 4
This attack table is composed of lethal weapons that can quickly annihilate an entire expedition.
The attacks found here are usually baked-in weapons and not malfunctioning peripherals.
The universal requirement is lethality.
Soft-touch attacks are not permitted when using Attack Table 4.

=== Disregard Fabricator
Any robot that reaches attack level 4 automatically gets to disregard their fabricator.
This offensive peripheral disengages any connection to the persona’s fabricator type.
Fabricator based commands do not weigh on this robot’s programming.

=== Aerosol
On Attack Table 4, an aerosol will be an actual weapon built into the robot.
The aerosol type rolled must be a killing aerosol.
No soft touch aerosols are allowed.
The aerosol can recharge after 1 minute of uncombat time.
To determine the aerosol type jump to xref:hardware:aerosols.adoc[Aerosols,window=_blank, rel="noopener noreferrer"].


=== Grenade
This attack employs a self-launching lethal grenade attack.
There is a minimum lethality required for the grenade.
No soft touch grenades are allowed.
The grenade must have a killing attack.

The player must win a Fling attack roll to hit the target hex.
Like all grenades, close does count.
The grenade is an area of effect weapon damaging, targets and colleagues alike.
The attack has a range of 2 hexes per point of Strength.
To determine the grenade type jump to xref:hardware:grenades.adoc#_grenade_type[Grenades,window=_blank, rel="noopener noreferrer"].

=== Lethal Jet
The lethal jet is a killing poison attack.
This attack is a baked-in intentional killing tool that the robot ejects from its chassis.
The lethal jet only works on organic targets.
The jet is a Shoot attack, and the player must win an attack roll for the poison to take.

A robot cannot have more than one lethal jet attack.
A second lethal jet should be replaced by a different Attack Table 4 result.

.*Range*
The range of the attack is 1 hex per point of her Strength score.
A robot with a 13 STR has a  lethal jet range of 13 hexes.

.*Intensity*
The intensity of the attack is different each time it is activated.
The initial poison attack intensity is 1-8 (1d8) per point of prime STR.
A robot with a prime STR of 3 has a poison intensity of 3-24.

The organic target must win a poison roll or die.
Anthros that lose their poison roll fall unconscious and have -1 hit points.
Aliens that lose their poison roll fall unconscious and have 1 hit points.
Anthros and aliens are dead in 10 units without intervention.

Organic targets that win their saving throw take 1-4 hit points (1d4) of damage per point of poison intensity.
If this damage proves lethal, the persona may switch her saving throw to a loss.

=== Combat Mutation
The robot will have a baked-in weapon that functions like a mutation.
The mutation must be a combat mutation.
The mutation cannot be a defect or compromise the persona in any way.

Robots have a 0 Mind score.
The player uses the robot’s Intelligence score for mutations that depend on Mind.

The mutation type can be a Psionic Mutation or a Phenomic Mutation.
If the player rolls less than her constitution score on 1d100, the attack has a xref:mutations:phenomics.adoc#_combat[Phenomic Mutation,window=_blank, rel="noopener noreferrer"] effect.
If the 1d100 roll is greater than her constitution score, the attack has a xref:mutations:psionics.adoc#_combat[Psionic Mutation,window=_blank, rel="noopener noreferrer"] effect.
