
= Defence

[quote]
____
*The best defence is the best defence.*
____

Defence (DEF) is a universal measure of how difficult something is to damage in combat.
Defence is a collection of hide thickness, Dexterity, armour and luck.
Defence represents a single number that determines how difficult a target is to hit and damage.
The higher the Defence, the more difficult it is to damage the persona.

An attack roll must be greater than, or equal to, the target’s Defence to do damage.

== Naked Defence
The naked Defence is the persona’s Defence when wearing no protection at all.
For aliens and robots it the only Defence that they know.
For anthros naked Defence means wearing no armour, it does not mean running around nude.
Armour increases the persona’s Defence.

.*Persona Defence Value*
* *Anthro Defence* = 500 + 6 x DEX
** Anthro, DEX 15 = 500 + 90 = 590
** Increased by wearing armour
* *Alien Defence* = 500 + 3d100 (random)
** Alien Roll 242 = 500 + 242 = 742
** Hide type has no effect.
* *Robot Defence* = 700
** Only adjusted by peripherals

The persona’s Defence includes all the dekes and dodges that one would use to avoid getting damaged in combat.
Extra descriptions of the perfect defensive move can earn EXPS but do not alter Defence.

== Armoured Defence
Armouring up is the process of wearing specialized clothing to decrease the chance of being damaged in combat.
Armour deflects, absorbs and distributes the damaging forces.
The goal of armour is to enhance the persona’s naked Defence making them harder to damage.

=== Anthros
The armour section is anthro-centric.
Anthro personas will almost always be wearing armour when on an expedition.
Armour, especially mundane armour, is restrictive and heavy.
Restrictive armour can slow the persona down, impair her task rolls and impair initiative.

=== Aliens
Most aliens do not wear clothing, let alone armour.
Their naked Defence is their natural Defence.
Armour will rarely be better than their hides and natural defences.

Only tool-using aliens can wear armour.
An alien that is a tool-user may have bespoke armour fashioned for her.
None tool-using aliens (wildlife) would not understand or tolerate armour.

==== Alien Armour Wate
Armour wate is based on a 70 kg anthro.
So a gigantic alien armour would be very heavy indeed.
The referee must adjust the wate of alien armour as below.

.*Alien Armour Wate Adjustment*
. Multiplier = alien wate kg / 70 kg
. Armour wate = anthro wate x Multiplier
. Leather armour for a 350 kg alien is 15 kg.

=== Robots
Robots do not wear armour.
A robot may have defensive systems that improve her Defence or function like artifact armour.

=== Does it Fit?
Armour bought by the persona will fit.
However, armour found while adventuring will not necessarily fit.
Before armour can fit, it must meet 2 criteria: shape and size.
The previous owner must have the same body shape, and the previous owner’s wate must be within 15%.

An anthro could not wear the bespoke armour of a four-armed avarian tripod alien.
If a persona’s wate is 70 kgs, the previous owner’s wate must be between 59.5 and 80.5 kg.

=== Suiting Up
Suiting up for battle is can be time-consuming.
The more restrictive the armour type, the longer it takes to put on the armour.
Without help and under no pressure, mundane armour takes 1.5 minutes per point of Binding to put on.
Biomech uses its own xref:hardware:biomech.adoc#_suiting_up[Suiting Up] rules.
A suit of chain mail would take about 8 minutes to put on.

=== Maintenance
Well-maintained armour should never wear out.
Attending to armour after a battle and in a scheduled manner should keep things in working order.
Inhospitable environments will destroy armour as quickly as any other mundane equipment.

Unattended armour will damage.
Whatever damage that the persona takes, the armour will take also.
Armour has 1 hit point per point of DEF.
A suit of unmaintained studded armour could withstand 575 hit points of damage before being wrecked.
At 0 hit points, the armour loses Defence and structural integrity.

=== Mix and Match
Scavenging personas will often mix up armour types.
A persona may have a synthetix helmet, metal breastplate, padded arms and studded leggings.
The only way that The referee can run mix and match armour is by using the xref:combat:hit_locations.adoc[Hit Locations,window=_blank, rel="noopener noreferrer"].
The ref determines where the attack will land and then rolls to hit against the appropriate Defence.

=== Accessories
Every suit of armour has a matching set of gauntlets and a helmet.
The helmet and gauntlets are composed of the same material as the body armour.
Padded armour would have a padded hat and padded gloves.

Helmets and gauntlets offer no Defence bonus.
Not wearing a helmet or gauntlets does not affect the Defence bonus.
Like in the movies, personas can engage in combat with the same Defence with or without a helmet.
If players choose to leave their persona’s noggins unprotected, the hazards of debris and vehicle crashes are worse.

== Shields
Shielding is any non-garment device used to reduce the chance of being hit.
Shielding deflects blows and reduces her exposure to damage.
Shielding requires the persona to use up a proficiency to use it properly.
Whichever arm the player is using to manipulate the shielding cannot do anything else.

== Binding
[[_restrictiveness]]Binding measures how much worn armour limits the persona’s freedom of movement.
The higher the Binding, the more cumbersome and unyielding the armour is.
Binding affects the Dexterity contribution to Defence and the armour’s contribution to encumbrance.
Wearing no armour has Binding 0.
Plate Armour has Binding 5.
Binding cannot be less than 0.
Biomech uses its powered or unpowered state instead of a numerical Binding rating.

=== Strength
Strength helps a persona carry armour through her Wate Allowance.
Use the armour’s full listed Binding when calculating its encumbrance contribution.

For the optional Dexterity adjustment below only, every 5 points of STR reduce the effective Binding by 1, to a minimum of 0.
A persona with STR 11 wearing Plate Armour has effective Binding 3 for this Defence calculation, but still uses Binding 5 for encumbrance.

=== Dexterity
Armour increases Defence, but Binding can reduce the Dexterity contribution to the persona’s naked Defence.
This adjustment is optional.
Subtract the Strength-adjusted Binding from the usual Dexterity multiplier of 6, to a minimum multiplier of 0.
Then add the armour’s Defence bonus as usual.
Binding does not reduce the Dexterity attribute itself.

A persona with DEX 10 and effective Binding 3 has naked Defence 530: 500 + (6 - 3) x 10.
The armour’s Defence bonus is added to 530.

=== Encumbrance
[[_movement]]Worn armour contributes its actual wate multiplied by its full listed Binding to the persona’s encumbrance load.
This effective load replaces the armour’s actual wate in the load calculation; do not count the armour twice.
Use the resulting encumbrance category to determine movement and initiative effects.
There is no separate Binding movement penalty.
Strong personas can carry a greater effective load, while weaker personas may be unable to move in heavy armour.
For the formula and examples, see xref:uncombat:encumbrance.adoc#_worn_armour[Worn Armour].

=== Task Rolls
Armour affects task rolls through the encumbrance category produced by its effective load.
Use the xref:uncombat:encumbrance.adoc#_tasks[Encumbrance and Task Rolls] rules.
Do not add a separate DD penalty per point of Binding.
Removing the armour changes the load and may improve the persona’s encumbrance category.
Specific armour descriptions may impose additional limitations, such as an obscuring visor or bulky gauntlets.
