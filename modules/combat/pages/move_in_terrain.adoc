= Terrain Combat

The surface of the land also influences movement in combat.
A persona can move faster over solid ground than through a knee-deep swamp.
The terrain surfaces covered here are variations on typical environments.
Combat movement covers the mundane terrains.
For exotic terrains like zero gravity or exatmo, jump to  xref:uncombat:terrains.adoc[Exotic Terrain,window=_blank, rel="noopener noreferrer"].

== Foliage
The degree to which grass, plants fungus patches affect movement depends on the persona’s size.
Tiny, Small, Medium, Large, and Gigantic personas can overcome specific foliage depths.
Anthro personas are Medium-sized.
Foliage movement costs affect aliens, robots and anthros equally.
Some aliens can fly over the foliage.
Some robots can float over the foliage.
Locomotion differences and foliage movement costs are up to referee and player debate.
If the debate becomes heated, this would be an excellent opportunity to use xref:uncombat:sphincter_rolls.adoc[Sphincter Roll,window=_blank, rel="noopener noreferrer"].

Regular grass is not a manicured lawn in suburbia.
Suburban lawns are, at worst low grass.
The foliage table is for flora that could impede a persona’s movement.

If the foliage penalty reduces a persona’s movement rate below 1 h/u, she must stop.
The foliage has become too thick for regular combat movement.
Minute or Nano personas can usually navigate through foliage with no effect until Mess or Wall depth.
Colossal or Humongous creatures can pass over foliage with impunity.

== Liquid
Water, or any liquid substance, slows down personas is covered here.
Aliens with water movement rates are not affected by swimming and use their natural movement.
The liquid table applies to snow, sand, mud or Swedish retail ball pits.
When Sink appears on the table, the persona can no longer wade through the liquid.
They then must swim, sink or stick depending on the liquid.

If the liquid penalty reduces a persona’s movement rate below 1 h/u, she can no longer wade.
Use <<_sink_or_swim,Sink or Swim>> to determine whether she swims or sinks.
Nano and Tiny sized personas must use the Sink or Swim rules at any depth.
Humongous and Colossal sized personas must use the Sink or Swim rules at Way Deep.

=== Sink or Swim
Whether a persona sinks or swims depends heavily on the family type.
An alien that has a water movement rate is unaffected by liquids.
An aquarian anthro can automatically swim.
Other anthros may or may not swim.
Check those xref:uncombat:sphincter_rolls.adoc[Sphincter Roll,window=_blank, rel="noopener noreferrer"] if the player can’t come up with a good back story.
Robots will automatically sink unless they have a water compensating locomotion type.

Those persons who can swim need only contend with wate encumbrance, currents, and sea monsters.
Only personas with evolved swimming abilities (aquarians and aliens) can make attack rolls while swimming.

The consequences of sinking are up to the referee.
Drowning is an unseemly way to force players to create new personas.
However, personas who cannot swim and are in the water are in trouble.
Water immersion will damage any robot lacking terrain or exatmo hardening.
A flailing anthro will have to shed armour, backpack and toys.

Alien and anthros that cannot swim can flail about once they dump their equipment.
Flailing can move the persona to the next water hex if a STR roll is successful.
Robots do not flail, they sink.

== Mud
Muddy liquid could be mud, slime, jello or any other dense liquid.
Mud uses the Liquid Movement Cost Table.
The thickness of the liquid increases its resistance and increases the depth by 2 levels.
So Knee-deep mud functions as Waist-deep water.
An anthro persona (Medium) would have a -4 h/u penalty in mud.
Instead of Sink, the persona is Stuck.
So if the persona's movement rate is below 1 h/u, or the table indicates Sink the persona is “stuck in the mud.”
Usually aliens cannot use their water movement in mud unless it is part of their biome.

== Sand
Packed sand is not a terrain obstacle.
The sand described here is shifting, sinking swirling sand.
The hex cost for moving through sand uses the Foliage Movement Cost Table.
To calculate the movement cost of sand, use foliage table, but increase the cost by 2 levels.
Ankle-deep sand uses the Legs row of the foliage table.
A Medium-sized persona in ankle-deep sand has a movement penalty of -2 h/u.
If the table result is Stop, or the penalty reduces movement below 1 h/u, the persona is stuck in the sand.

== Snow
Snow or other light particulate matter uses the Liquid Movement Cost Table.
To simulate snow, the referee uses the liquid movement cost and increases the cost by 1 level.
So Knee-deep snow has the movement cost of Leg deep water.
A medium-sized persona would have a 3 hex cost penalty in knee-deep snow.
If the table result is Sink, or the penalty reduces movement below 1 h/u, the persona is stuck in a snowbank.

== Ice
Ice, or any frictionless surface, is a source of slippery fun for referees.
Unless the persona has special tires, claws or skills, the frictionless surface will wreak havoc.
Ice will offer a 1d4 - 1 hex cost for all personas for every combat unit on the ice.
Some personas will be unable to move on ice and use their entire movement rate to avoid falling over.

Whether ice over liquid can support larger personas must be decided by the referee in advance.
If the cracking ice is an off the cuff terrain modification the xref:uncombat:sphincter_rolls.adoc[Sphincter Roll,window=_blank, rel="noopener noreferrer"] come into play.

== Climbing
Vertical movement that does not involve flying, telekinesis or antigrav requires climbing.
Personas engaged in climbing make far more predictable targets than otherwise.
Attack rolls on climbing targets gain bonuses.
Attack rolls made by climbing personas suffer penalties.

NOTE: *Outside of combat personas just climb things.*

Robots do not climb.
Alien wildlife does not climb.
Tool using aliens and anthros may try their paws at climbing.
Climbing cannot be combined with Running or Bolting.

.*Climbing Upwards*
. Find the hold, AWE roll
. Reach the hold, DEX roll
. Advance 1-2 hexes that unit, STR roll

The persona can move in any desired direction: up, down, sideways.

=== Walls Free
Free climbing is free of ropes, pitons, and carabiners.
The referee determines if a surface can be free climbed.
Free climbing requires all the persona’s attention.
The player cannot combine free climbing with attack rolls or unrelated task rolls.
Attack rolls made on a free climbing persona enjoy an attack roll bonus of +666.

.*Free Climbing*
* must beat 18 on each attribute roll.
* failure of DEX or STR indicates a fall.

The consequences of falling are up to the referee.
The fall is harmless, but the sudden stop at the bottom inflicts 1d4 damage per hex of fall.

=== Walls Roped
Equipped climbing uses ropes, pitons, and carabiners.
Setting up climbing equipment cannot happen during combat.
This section is used if the persona gets into combat while doing a roped climb.
Equipped climbing requires all the persona’s attention.
The persona can choose to not climb and make an attack roll or do hands free task roll.
Attack rolls made on a roped climbing persona enjoy an attack roll bonus of +333.
Attack rolls made by a roped climbing persona suffer an attack roll penalty of -160

.*Rope Climbing*
* must beat 10 on each attribute roll.
* failure indicates no movement, not a fall.

=== Ropes
This is for climbing with just a rope and no other equipment.
This would be when the persona is using just a rope to climb out of a pit, or up a wall.
A persona can climb upwards at 1 h/u, or downwards at 2 h/u.
If they are targeted they suffer the same attack roll adjustments as free climbing above.


=== Ladders
Climbing by ladder requires no attribute rolls.
A persona may climb upwards at 1d2 h/u, or  downwards at 1d2+1 h/u.
These rules only apply during combat, so the situation is likely dangerous enough already.
Unless there are extenuating circumstances, the referee should allow personas to navigate ladders safely.
If they are targeted they suffer the same attack roll adjustments as roped climbing above.

== Hills
This guide refers to hills one finds on regular terrain.
If a hill is too steep it becomes a wall.
A persona cannot Run or Bolt up a hill (apologies to https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Running_Up_That_Hill[Kate Bush,window=_blank, rel="noopener noreferrer"]).
A persona can make attack rolls as normal on a hill.

.*Climbing*
There is a -1d2 h/u penalty when climbing up a typical hill.
This is rolled every unit.
If the persona's movement rate is less than 1 h/u the persona stays still on the hill that unit.

.*Descending*
To safely descend a hill the player suffers the same movement penalty of -1d2 h/u.
If a persona wants to scamper down a hill they earn a descent bonus  +1d2 h/u, but must beat 10 on a DEX roll.
If they fail the roll, they face-plant.
A face-plant leaves the persona unable to move or act that unit.
